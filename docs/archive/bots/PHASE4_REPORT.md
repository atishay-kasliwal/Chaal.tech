### Challengers

- **Dhoni `ms-dhoni@7-blindcost`** (tp-dna@3 plus one base factor: looking gets more attractive as the blind stake climbs). Fixes the exploit (+1,153 ± 45 boots/100 against always-raise, +251 ± 84 against short-stack attacks), passes regression, personality, strength (-78 ± 169 overall), variety, bad actions and performance, but **fails the counter-exploit gate**: against "value-then-bluff" it is worse by 802 ± 510 (looking earlier, he folds more seen hands to the later bluffs). **Not recommended as is.** A Phase 4.1 refinement would scale the factor by how loose this table's blind raisers are believed to be (Dhoni's reads), so it acts against maniacs and not against a tight player who turns.
- **Samay** (event reactions on tp-dna@6). The quirk form (`remembersYou`, 0.6 to 1.5) changes P(pack) by only about -0.012 wherever it acts, whatever the strength: the quirk cap and the clarity bound absorb it, which is why Phase 3.1's event quirks were invisible. The read form (`remembersYouRead`) scales (-0.08 / -0.15 / -0.22) and at 1.0 passes the exploit gate, but loses target bands (4/9 vs 6/9) and raises bad actions. **None recommended**: making Samay's memory visible costs his calling-station identity. A future version should put remembered bluffs into his opponent read (bluffShown) rather than a separate push.

### Dhoni's adaptation

He adapts in the right direction where evidence is plentiful (bluffing rocks: +0.217 [0.192, 0.247], the only large learning effect of any character; stops bluffing stations, slightly), but against maniacs, value players and both style switchers the effects are small and not distinguishable from zero over 200-hand sessions. Style switchers do not fool him into anything harmful, but he does not visibly notice the switch either.

### Variation and identity

Sessions differ for real: night-to-night variety is 139 to 182 (scaled) for the champions against 21 to 62 for their stateless versions. Moods drive most of it for Rohit, Virat and Dhoni; GG's disciplined mood barely varies (12 ± 11). Across typical nights each character stays recognisable: 86% (Dhoni) to 99% (Samay) of whole sessions are identified as the right character.

### Measurement lesson

Short sessions on many tables measure 3 to 8 times more precisely than long ones; 12 nights (Phase 3.1's gate) was far too few for night variety (17 to 42% error). Phase 4 used 50 to 100.

**What this decided.** Paired (matched) play cuts the spread by a third to two thirds. Shorter sessions are far more efficient per hand: a small difference in play compounds through a long session (stacks diverge), so the same 200 hands as four 50-hand sessions measure a difference 3 to 8 times more precisely. Strength comparisons in Phase 4 therefore use many short sessions (100 hands; 50 where no session effect is studied), and only adaptation studies keep 200-hand sessions. Against calling stations and maniacs the spread is so large that a ±100 interval needs 0.2 to 1.8 million hands per comparison: those intervals are reported as measured, and never treated as decisive. An exploit gap compares the champion with a different player (the reference), so it cannot be paired and is the noisiest measure of all.

## 12. Engineering

- Simulated: **11,951,600 hands, 264,007,333 decisions** across all Phase 4 stages in this directory, in 5.63 hours of wall time on 7 worker processes (589 hands/s overall). Peak worker memory 200 MB.
- Determinism: every table is a pure function of its spec (seed, table, seats, hands, decision seed, night key); `tests/server/bot-phase4.test.mjs` replays adversary tables bit for bit, and the champions' decision digests are locked (`tests/server/bot-baseline.test.mjs`).
- Fairness: adversaries and archetypes decide from their own projection only (a test swaps another player's unseen cards and gets the same decision); minds are built from the bot's own projections (existing swap tests); event attribution reads the deciding bot's own mind offline only; bluff evidence comes only from revealed cards; nothing is shared between seats.
- Offline only: tp-dna@6 and mind@4 are registered for replay and simulation; no published or canonical style uses them (test). Nothing was published, merged or adopted.
